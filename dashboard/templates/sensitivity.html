Experiment 4 — how much does the answer depend on how we asked?
The other three experiments ask how fast, how large and how accurate each simulator is. This one asks something different: whether those answers survive a change in the question. A benchmark whose ranking flips when the network size or the metric changes has not produced a recommendation, it has produced an artefact of its own configuration — and knowing which of the two you are holding is the point.
The statistic is Kendall's τ, the rank correlation between two orderings of the same backends. τ = 1 means the two orderings agree exactly, 0 means they are unrelated, and −1 means one is the reverse of the other.
Rank order: smallest network measured vs largest
One line per backend, connecting its speed rank (1 = fastest) at the smallest network size to its rank at the largest. A flat line means that backend's position held; a line that crosses many others means it did not.
Does the ordering depend on the network size measured?
Each tile compares the runtime ordering at that network size against the ordering at the largest size measured.
Does the ordering depend on the metric chosen?
Each tile compares the ordering under that metric against the ordering by runtime, both at the largest network size.
Objective O2 — the energy proxy against the EFLOP metric
The stated objective is to assess how far the utilisation-weighted energy proxy agrees with an operation-count metric, by rank correlation.
